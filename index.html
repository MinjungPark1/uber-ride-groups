02 · THE CONCEPT

One person books.
Everyone has a say.

THE ORGANIZER’S JOB

Make the organizer’s job lighter.

“Booking with Uber means less work for me.”

When friends compare ride options, the person handling the reservation also carries the coordination: who is joining, which car fits, and who agrees to pay what.

SCENARIO · CURRENT GROUP DECISION

The agreement lives in chat.

Friends compare apps and prices. One friend organizes the ride, then waits until after matching to use an equal fare split—or handles a different allocation outside that flow.

Illustrative journey, grounded in Uber’s documented split timing.
PROPOSED CHECKOUT

The agreement lives with the ride.

Followed friends get an invitation showing their exact cents. Each person says yes before the organizer requests a vehicle.

Intended usability gain: less coordination for the friend who books.
01

Invite at checkout

The organizer selects accounts they already follow. No one gets added to a bill without an invitation.

02

Show exact shares

The group sees allocations down to the cent. A revised amount requires a fresh approval.

03

Wait for each yes

Every rider authorizes only their own amount. The lead requests the car after all selected riders approve.

04

Finish separately

In this simulated flow, individual charges happen after the ride. An unpaid share never shifts automatically to the organizer.

04 · BUSINESS IMPACT

Less work for the lead.
A better chance to win the ride.

The business case starts with a group choosing an app together. Easier coordination could make Uber the option the organizer proposes—and other riders accept.

A · GROUP CHOICE

Win group occasions

When friends compare apps, a clear checkout may reduce hesitation. More groups choosing Uber would mean incremental trips; existing Uber trips displaced by the new flow must be counted separately.

Test: group booking conversion
B · ACTIVE RIDERS

Bring friends into the ride

Invitations reach followed accounts that already exist. This may activate occasional riders or increase their usage; new-account growth would require a separate onboarding path.

Test: invited-rider activation
C · SPENDING

Make options easier to weigh

Seeing a personal share might make a larger vehicle or extra stop feel worth considering. Higher total trip value is a hypothesis, and the group should still see the full price.

Test: group trip value and margin
ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO · NOT UBER PRICES

Four friends choose together.

A $32 trip is $8 each. If they choose a roomier $48 option, it becomes $12 each—$4 more per person and $16 more in total fare. Whether people actually upgrade, and what Uber earns from it, needs testing.

What would prove it?

Compare group checkout completion, organizer repeat bookings, invited-rider activity, total fare per group occasion, contribution margin, and support contacts against the current flow. A larger fare is not the same as more Uber revenue or profit.
